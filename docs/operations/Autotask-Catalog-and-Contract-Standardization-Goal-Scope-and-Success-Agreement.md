## 1. Goal

Establish an accurate, consistent, maintainable Autotask catalog of Products, Services, and Service Bundles, followed by standardized customer Contracts and reliable billing reconciliation.

The catalog shall support purchasing, inventory, quoting, service delivery, contract administration, and billing.

Use native Autotask capabilities wherever practical. This workstream does not automatically supersede other Project Jason priorities.

## 2. Core Operating Principle

**Inventory → Establish standards → Correct and validate records → Build reusable bundles → Align contracts → Reconcile and automate.**

Preserve historical relationships and existing business commitments.

Use authoritative evidence rather than assumptions.

Apply standards progressively, without unnecessary mass renaming or replacement of legacy records.

Unresolved issues shall not prevent unrelated authorized work.

## 3. Six-Phase Work Plan

### Phase 1 — Inventory and Audit

Inventory all active and inactive Products, Services, Service Bundles, and relevant Contract relationships.

Identify duplicate candidates, incomplete information, naming inconsistencies, questionable financial data, inactive records, and operational dependencies.

Prioritize active records and inactive records with current dependencies for subsequent standardization.

**Acceptance:** Complete accounting of the accessible population, verified through provider evidence, record counts and appropriate pagination. Document dependencies, discrepancies, and retrieval gaps.

### Phase 2 — Standardize Products

Establish approved standards for product names, descriptions, categories, manufacturers, vendors, models, part numbers, purchase costs, selling prices, margins, tax treatment, and inventory tracking.

Apply standards when creating, replacing, or appropriately updating Products under authorized plans.

**Acceptance:** Every record in the approved review population is evaluated. Every executed correction is verified, with remaining discrepancies tracked.

### Phase 3 — Standardize Services

Establish standards for managed services, subscriptions, professional services, recurring and nonrecurring charges, billing units, vendors, costs, prices, and quantity methodologies.

**Acceptance:** Every Service in the approved population is evaluated. Billing methodology, applicable financial information, and executed corrections are verified or documented as exceptions.

### Phase 4 — Build Standardized Service Bundles

Create or improve native Service Bundles from validated underlying Services.

Verify composition, descriptions, quantities, costs, prices, and margins.

**Acceptance:** Each approved bundle demonstrates its intended composition and functional behavior. Unverified financial information is not presented as verified.

### Phase 5 — Standardize Contracts

Evaluate selected customer Contracts against validated catalog records and bundles.

Identify incorrect assignments, duplicate charges, quantity discrepancies, pricing exceptions, billing frequencies, and renewal information.

Preserve customer-specific commitments unless explicitly authorized modifications apply.

**Acceptance:** Every Contract in the approved review population receives a documented outcome. Authorized corrections are verified and customer obligations remain protected.

### Phase 6 — Reconcile and Automate

Implement governed reconciliation between contracted Services, bundles, assigned licenses, actual usage, billing records, and invoices.

Prefer native provider functionality and use Jason for cross-system evidence, exception management, and approved corrective workflows.

**Acceptance:** All mandatory representative billing scenarios in the approved acceptance plan pass, including discrepancy detection and authorization-boundary tests.

## 4. Data Quality and Naming

AOT shall establish category-specific naming and data-quality conventions informed by relevant standards, including ISO 8000, ISO 22745, ISO/IEC 20000-1, and GS1 where applicable.

These standards inform AOT policy; they do not imply certification or establish a universal ISO naming convention.

**No mandatory mass renaming.** Existing names shall generally be retained. Approved conventions shall apply to new records, replacements, and authorized updates involving names.

Fields shall be categorized as required, conditionally required, or optional.

Values must be verified, legitimately not applicable, or identified as unresolved exceptions.

Manufacturer identifiers, part numbers, models, and other applicable information shall use appropriate native Autotask fields.

## 5. Duplicate Treatment

**Agreed: Establish preferred records, preserve history, and retire duplicates only when safe and approved.**

Jason shall identify possible duplicates using record identifiers, descriptions, vendor information, classifications, units, and functional characteristics.

Similar names alone are insufficient evidence of duplication.

Preferred records shall be identified for future use.

Historical references shall remain intact.

Superseded records may be retired only through supported native operations after dependency verification and authorization.

Duplicate identification does not authorize deletion or rewriting historical transactions.

## 6. Financial Data Authority

**Agreed: Authoritative vendor costs and approved AOT selling prices.**

Vendor costs shall be supported by authoritative evidence, accounting for applicable units, billing periods, dates, and currencies.

Standard selling prices shall follow approved AOT pricing policies.

Calculated prices, margins, and markups do not independently authorize price changes.

Existing customer-specific Contract pricing shall be preserved unless changes are expressly authorized.

### Unverifiable Costs

When authoritative costs are unavailable or conflicting:

- Preserve existing financial values.
- Record the missing or conflicting evidence.
- Create or update a financial-verification exception.
- Do not represent unverified costs or margins as accurate.
- Continue unrelated eligible work.
- Track the exception through verification or an authorized disposition.

Resolving missing information does not independently authorize changing financial records.

## 7. Phase-Based Authorization

**Agreed: Exact correction plans approved by phase.**

Each phase shall follow:

1. Analyze relevant records and establish the baseline.
2. Prepare an exact correction plan specifying records, permitted changes, dependencies, financial effects, risks, verification, and recovery.
3. Obtain applicable human authorization.
4. Execute changes within approved boundaries.
5. Verify results through provider readback and dependency checks.
6. Report acceptance, completion measurements, and unresolved exceptions.

Approval of one phase does not automatically authorize another.

Material changes outside an approved plan require additional applicable authorization.

Production deployment remains subject to separate exact-revision approval under Project Jason governance.

## 8. Approval Authority

### 8.1 Agreed Governance Model

AOT management may receive delegated authority to approve nonfinancial catalog correction plans.

The Project Owner retains authority over financial, contractual, and production changes.

### 8.2 Interim Approval Assignment

**Agreed: The AOT Project Owner, Al Davis, will serve as the Interim Catalog Approval Authority.**

Until further delegation is formally established, the Project Owner shall approve all phase correction plans, including nonfinancial catalog corrections.

This assignment defines the intended approval responsibility. It does not itself configure permissions or create executable approval grants in Jason.

### 8.3 Future Delegation

The Project Owner may subsequently designate qualified AOT management personnel to approve defined categories of nonfinancial corrections.

Delegations must identify the approved individuals or roles, permitted operations, scope, limitations, and applicable authorization requirements.

Changes to delegation shall be documented and governed.

Delegation may be modified or revoked without rewriting the underlying six-phase workstream agreement, provided the approved governance model remains unchanged.

### 8.4 Reserved Owner Authority

The Project Owner retains approval authority over:

- Catalog selling-price and material cost-related corrections.
- Customer-specific pricing, discounts, and financial terms.
- Contract modifications and customer obligations.
- Billing and invoice changes.
- Material financial exposure.
- Production releases and deployments.
- Changes to delegated approval authority.

Changes with material financial or contractual consequences must not be treated as nonfinancial merely because the modified field appears administrative.

### 8.5 Approval Mechanism

Approvals shall use existing trusted identity and governed approval mechanisms.

Each approval must bind to the exact authorized work.

No delegation permits unrestricted catalog modification or bypass of existing governance.

## 9. Acceptance and Exception Management

**Agreed: Phases may be accepted with documented noncritical exceptions. Critical exceptions preventing safe operation block acceptance.**

**Accepted:** Required work and verification are complete, with no outstanding exceptions.

**Accepted with Exceptions:** Required work and applicable verification are complete. Every remaining noncritical exception has an owner, disposition, evidence, next action, and review date.

**Not Accepted:** Required work or verification remains incomplete, or a critical exception prevents safe operation.

Exceptions remain tracked after acceptance.

Jason shall distinguish execution completion, data-quality coverage, financial verification, functional acceptance, and exception status.

## 10. Phase-Specific Completion Measurement

**Agreed: Phase-specific measurable criteria, rather than a universal catalog-compliance percentage.**

Each phase shall establish its baseline, record population, applicable standards, numerical acceptance targets, verification requirements, and permitted exceptions before its correction plan is approved.

Phase 1 requires complete accounting of the accessible inventory.

Phases 2 and 3 require complete evaluation of approved populations and verification of executed corrections.

Phase 4 requires functional verification of approved bundles.

Phase 5 requires documented review of selected Contracts and verification of authorized changes.

Phase 6 requires all mandatory acceptance scenarios to pass.

Excluded legacy records and documented inaccessible populations must not distort completion measurements.

Successful code execution, passing narrow tests, or a completed deployment does not independently establish operational completion.

## 11. Ongoing Catalog Maintenance

**Agreed: Continuous advisory validation, monthly audits, and quarterly comprehensive reviews.**

### 11.1 Continuous Advisory Validation

Jason shall evaluate new and updated catalog records against approved AOT standards through authorized capabilities.

**Validation is advisory only.** It shall not block otherwise permitted record creation or updates.

Findings may produce recommendations, exceptions, and notifications, but not unauthorized modifications.

Native Autotask validation and Project Jason security controls remain applicable.

### 11.2 Monthly Audits

Once per calendar month, Jason shall review new and changed records and outstanding exceptions.

Reviews shall examine duplicates, missing information, outdated vendor costs, pricing discrepancies, relationship changes, and overdue follow-ups.

Reports shall distinguish new findings, existing exceptions, resolved issues, and proposed corrections.

### 11.3 Quarterly Comprehensive Reviews

Once per calendar quarter, Jason shall conduct a read-only review of active and inactive catalog records.

Reviews shall evaluate inventory coverage, duplicate groups, preferred records, applicable field completeness, financial verification, relationships, and outstanding exceptions.

Completeness claims must be supported by provider evidence and pagination verification.

### 11.4 Corrective Action

Audit findings do not authorize modifications.

Jason may prepare correction plans for approval under the applicable delegated or reserved authority.

### 11.5 Maintenance Verification

The maintenance program shall be considered operational only after the approved implementation demonstrates accurate detection, exception tracking, reporting, and governed correction planning through its intended operational paths.

## 12. Definition of Success

The workstream succeeds when all six phases satisfy their agreed measurable acceptance requirements, with permitted noncritical exceptions documented.

Historical relationships must remain intact.

Authorized modifications must be traceable to approved plans.

Products, Services, Bundles, and Contracts must function correctly through representative native Autotask workflows.

Billing reconciliation must produce accurate, explainable results.

Continuous advisory validation, monthly audits, quarterly reviews, and governed correction planning must be operationally verified.

Unresolved critical exceptions preventing safe operation prohibit acceptance.

Success does not require renaming every legacy record, deleting every historical duplicate, or immediately resolving every noncritical exception.

## 13. Scope and Priority Control

This agreement establishes a separate Autotask standardization workstream.

It does not automatically supersede approved engineering work, production reliability, ticket operations, or other Project Jason priorities.

Unrelated enhancements shall be addressed separately.

## 14. Agreement Approval Boundary

Approval establishes the workstream's objectives, principles, six-phase sequence, financial and data-quality standards, approval model, acceptance requirements, and maintenance schedule.

It does not independently authorize provider modifications, financial changes, new permissions, or production deployment.

Each exact correction plan and production release requires its applicable authorization.
